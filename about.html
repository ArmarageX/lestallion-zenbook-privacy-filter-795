Angle & Edge publishes a focused issue about laptop-screen privacy as a problem of viewing direction, physical fit, and everyday use. Our editorial boundary

The feature and its six guides begin with checks a reader can perform on their own device: identify the model suffix, measure the active picture, inspect the camera and bezel, verify lid clearance, and consider who can see the screen from nearby seats. A compatibility label alone cannot determine whether mounting parts fit safely. A dark-looking oblique photograph cannot establish that a person standing behind the owner cannot read a heading. The guides keep such distinctions visible rather than turning them into a product ranking.

The main feature links once to a separately operated commerce article. That destination is an additional place to research products, not a measurement record from this site or an endorsement of a particular accessory. We have not bought the listed filters, measured their optical performance, tested a named ZenBook configuration, or established availability and pricing. Check the current manufacturer's dimensions, care instructions, and return conditions before making a decision. A seller's changing claim should not silently become an editorial fact here.

How to read the issue

Start with the measuring guide if a listing says only “14 inch.” The mounting guide focuses on the attachment's route around bezel, camera, and hinge. The sight-line guide separates adjacent seating from a direct rear view; the display-comfort guide asks whether the owner's view remains useful.
